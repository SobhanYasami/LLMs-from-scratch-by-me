Aim. Meet the transformer (encoder and decoder), see how BERT and GPT each use half of it, and learn zero-shot versus few-shot use.

The original transformer

Introduced in the 2017 paper Attention Is All You Need for machine translation. It has two parts:

Input text"This is an example"
→
Encoderturns text into vectors that capture context
→
Decodergenerates the output one word at a time
→
Output"Das ist ein Beispiel"

Both halves are layers connected by self-attention: a way for the model to weigh how important each token is relative to every other token. That gives it long-range context. Chapter 3 implements it. Chapter 2 prepares the inputs it eats.

BERT versus GPT

BERT: the encoder half

Trained on masked word prediction: guess hidden words inside a sentence. Strong at classification (sentiment, topic, toxic content).

GPT: the decoder half

Trained to generate: predict what comes next. Strong at completion, translation, summaries, writing and code. This is what the book builds.

Zero-shot and few-shot

  • Zero-shot: do a task never seen, with no examples in the prompt.
  • Few-shot: you show a handful of examples inside the prompt, and the model follows the pattern. No retraining.
"Transformer" and "LLM" are used interchangeably but are not the same. Transformers also do vision, and some LLMs use recurrent or convolutional designs, mostly chasing efficiency.
