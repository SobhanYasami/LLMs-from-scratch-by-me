2.1 Understanding word embeddings

Neural networks do math on continuous numbers. Words are categories, so they cannot be fed in directly. An embedding maps a discrete thing (a word, image, document) to a point in a continuous vector space.

  • Different data needs different embedding models: a text model cannot embed audio.
  • Word2Vec was an early method: words appearing in similar contexts get similar vectors, so "bird" words cluster apart from "city" words.
  • Vectors can be 2-D for plotting, but real models use hundreds or thousands of dimensions. GPT-2 small uses 768; the biggest GPT-3 uses 12,288. More dimensions capture more nuance at more compute cost.
  • LLMs learn their own embeddings as the first layer, updated during training, so they fit the data and task. (Sentence or document embeddings exist too, used for retrieval; not covered.)

The data

We use The Verdict by Edith Wharton (public domain), 20,479 characters, saved as assets/the-verdict.txt. Tiny on purpose, so everything runs in seconds on a laptop.

Summary of the chapter

  • Text becomes tokens, tokens become IDs, IDs become vectors.
  • Special tokens handle unknown words and document boundaries.
  • BPE (used by GPT-2 and GPT-3) breaks unknown words into sub-words, so it needs no unknown token.
  • A sliding window creates (input, target) pairs.
  • Embedding layers are lookup tables; position vectors are added so order is not lost.
