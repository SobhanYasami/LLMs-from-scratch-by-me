1.3 and 1.5: Pretraining, finetuning and data

Aim. Learn the two-stage recipe (pretrain, then finetune) and why training data scale matters.

Two stages

Raw textunlabelled, billions of words
→
Pretrainingnext-word prediction
→
Base / foundation modelcompletes text
→
Finetuningsmall labelled dataset
→
Assistant or classifier
  • Pretraining gives broad language knowledge. The result is a base or foundation model (GPT-3 is the classic example). It completes half-written sentences and can learn a new task from a few examples.
  • Finetuning adapts it with labelled data. Two common kinds: instruction finetuning (instruction and answer pairs) and classification finetuning (texts and class labels, like spam / not spam).

Why so much data? (1.5)

GPT-3 was pretrained on a mix of web crawl, books and Wikipedia, roughly 300 billion tokens sampled from the pool below. A token is the unit of text a model reads, roughly a word or punctuation mark. Chapter 2 is all about producing tokens.

DatasetWhatTokensShare of training
CommonCrawl (filtered)web crawl410 B60%
WebText2web crawl19 B22%

Pretraining is very expensive (GPT-3's estimated cloud cost was about $4.6 million). So this book trains a small model on a small text for learning, and shows how to load openly published weights instead of paying for pretraining.
