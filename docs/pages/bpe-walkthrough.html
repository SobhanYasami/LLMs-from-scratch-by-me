Aim. Watch the GPT-2 byte pair encoder work on real words, so the merge loop in bpe.c and bpe.zig stops being magic.

The book treats BPE as a library call. Here is what that call does. Each token in the GPT-2 vocabulary has a rank: its position in the order merges were learned. Encoding a word is the reverse: start from bytes and keep applying the lowest-rank merge available.

The loop

  1. Split the word into single bytes (one part per byte).
  2. For every neighbouring pair, glue the two parts and look the result up. If it is a known token, it has a rank.
  3. Merge the pair with the lowest rank. Repeat from step 2.
  4. Stop when no neighbouring pair forms a known token. The remaining parts are the tokens.

Trace: unbelievable

start    u n b e l i e v a b l e
merge 1  rank   293   le          u n b e l i e v a b [le]
merge 2  rank   397   ab          u n b e l i e v [ab] le
merge 3  rank   403   un          [un] b e l i e v ab le
merge 4  rank   417   el          un b [el] i e v ab le
merge 5  rank   494   ie          un b el [ie] v ab le
merge 6  rank   540   able        un b el ie v [able]
merge 7  rank  6667   bel         un [bel] ie v able
merge 8  rank 11203   iev         un bel [iev] able
result   [403, 6667, 11203, 540]   = un | bel | iev | able

Notice the ranks climb: common pieces (le, ab, un) merge first, rarer ones (iev) last. The final token IDs are just the ranks of the surviving parts.

Trace: an unknown word, Zorblax

start    Z o r b l a x
merge 1  rank   273   or          Z [or] b l a x
merge 2  rank   897   ax          Z or b l [ax]
merge 3  rank  2436   bl          Z or [bl] ax
result   [57, 273, 2436, 897]   = Z | or | bl | ax

This answers exercise 2.1 of the page on BPE: a made-up word becomes four ordinary tokens. No <|unk|> is needed because single bytes are always in the vocabulary.

Where the text is cut first

Before merging, text is split into pieces by a pattern (contractions, a word with its leading space, digits, symbols, whitespace). Merging never crosses a piece boundary, so " world" and "world" are different tokens. nextPiece / next_piece in the C and Zig files implements that pattern by hand.

Try it

  1. Pick a word with a common prefix (unhappiness). Predict the merges, then compare with the output of your tokenizer.
  2. Why does the encoder pick the lowest rank first, not the longest match? (Hint: it reproduces the merge order learned in training.)
Answer to 2BPE training merged the most frequent pair first. Replaying merges lowest-rank-first recreates exactly the segmentation the model saw during training, which is what its embeddings were learned for.
